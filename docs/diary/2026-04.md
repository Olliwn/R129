### April 1, 2026 -- Trunk Fuse 6 Investigation & Data Correction

**Location:** Oulu, Finland

**Event:** Investigated trunk fuse 6 (found blown 2026-03-30) to determine affected systems and likely root cause. Cross-referenced local data repository against internet sources. Discovered a significant data error.

*   **CRITICAL FINDING: Trunk fuse card mismatch.** The fuse designation card in the data repository (P/N 129 545 25 00, dated 15.12.99, sourced from BenzWorld as `trunk_fuses.pdf`) describes the **post-facelift F4 trunk fuse box** (9 blade fuses, used on ~09/1995-2002 R129s). AOK912 (1991, pre-facelift) has the **F20 Auxiliary Fuse Holder** with torpedo fuses. The assignments are different:
    *   **Post-facelift F4:** Fuse 5 = Central locking + Antenna + Trunk lamp (10A). Fuse 6 = ATA alarm (15A blue).
    *   **Pre-facelift F20:** Assignments unverified from factory documentation. Forum evidence (BenzWorld, 1995 SL500 thread) suggests Fuse 6 = Central locking (and possibly power antenna).

*   **Original diary attribution likely correct.** The 2026-03-30 entry said "Fuse #6 found BLOWN — likely root cause of dead PSE central locking." Internet cross-reference supports this for pre-facelift cars, so the original hypothesis stands, but with reduced confidence since the F20 assignment has not been verified from the ETM or fuse box cover.

*   **ATA/IRCL diagnosis needs recheck.** The "dead ATA module" diagnosis from March 18 and 26 (X11/4 Pin 11 static glow, no blink response) should be re-evaluated after fuse replacement. If ATA is powered by a trunk fuse on the pre-facelift car, the blown fuse may have caused the "unpowered module" appearance. The conclusion "Genuine module fault" (2026-03-26) was drawn while fuse 6 was already blown.

*   **Power antenna may also be affected.** Forum evidence suggests central locking and power antenna may share the same pre-facelift trunk fuse. The stuck-extended Hirschmann antenna (dead motor, no activity) may simply have been unpowered.

*   **Data repository updated:**
    *   `r129_data/data/fuse_box.yaml` -- Added warning about pre-facelift vs post-facelift trunk fuse boxes. Replaced generic TRUNK_F1/F2 entries with properly labeled F4 (post-facelift) assignments and an F20_FUSE6 entry with caveats.

    *   `docs/known_issues.md` -- Updated PSE, Power Antenna, and Parasitic Drain entries with fuse investigation findings.

### April 3, 2026 -- Session Results

**Location:** Oulu, Finland

#### 1. Registration Plates Installed ✅
- Plates mounted. One of the rear plate bolts had to be cut using the newly purchased DeWalt DCS438 cut-off tool.

#### 2. Trunk Fuse 6 Replaced -- Power Antenna Partially Alive ⚠️
- Replaced blown fuse 6 (8A) in F20 holder. **Fuse is holding — did not blow.**
- **Power antenna responded immediately.** The upper segments extend and retract correctly with the radio power on/off.
- **However, the two lowest (thickest) antenna segments remain stuck in the extended position.** The antenna motor runs and tries to retract them but eventually gives up.
- **Likely cause:** Dried lubricant / contamination inside the antenna mast tube preventing the lower segments from sliding freely. The Hirschmann automatic antenna relies on a nylon drive cord and lubricated telescoping segments — after years of sitting, the grease hardens and the thicker lower segments bind.
- **FIX APPLIED:** WD-40 penetrant + light manual assist freed the stuck lower segments. Antenna now extends and retracts fully. ✅ One segment is not perfectly smooth but does not get stuck.
- **Follow-up:** Apply silicone spray (aerosol) into the mast tube for long-term lubrication once available. WD-40 is a penetrant, not a lasting lubricant.

#### 3. X11/4 Diagnostic Re-test After Fuse 6 + ADS Code 14 Cleared ✅

#### 6. Battery CCA Test (Owon HDS242 Cranking Waveform) ⚠️
- **Setup:** Probes on battery terminals, 2.0V/div, 200ms/div, DC trigger at 12.4V.
- **Resting voltage:** ~12.4–12.5V (trickle-charged).
- **Cranking dip:** ~4V drop (almost 2 divisions), minimum approximately **8.5V** during crank.
- **Crank duration:** Very short — engine catches immediately.
- **Ambient temperature:** 3°C — cold soak increases battery internal resistance and engine oil viscosity, both worsening the cranking dip.
- **Assessment:** Dip to ~8.5V at 3°C is borderline but not as alarming as the same reading at 20°C would be. A warm-weather retest would likely show a significantly shallower dip. The battery may still have adequate life, especially with trickle charger support.
- **Action item:** Re-test in warmer conditions (~15–20°C) for a fair CCA comparison. Monitor through summer and decide on replacement before autumn/winter 2026.
- Waveform saved: `assets/image-2037b7ba-04dc-4d11-8c2f-01ae7dde2cc6.png`

#### 7. Seat Control Panel — Loose Bottom Clip Identified
- Both door seat control panels (P/N 129 820 71 10, "W.-Germany" original) were loose at the bottom.
- **Cause:** The front lower plastic locating clip has broken off (brittle plastic after 35 years). Metal mounting clips at the corners are intact.
- **Fix attempt 1:** Biltema double-sided tape — failed, does not hold.
- **Next plan:** Source proper automotive-grade double-sided trim tape (3M VHB or similar). Alternatively, cut away remaining plastic at the broken clip location and repurpose the existing screw mount point to hold a new fabricated clip. Low urgency.
- Photo saved: `assets/image-630cb290-f4cd-4d8c-ac84-bc7788e69acf.png`

#### 8. V-Belt Squeal — Confirmed with Friction Spray ⚠️

- **Diagnosis:** Belt is glazed/worn and slipping under load. Friction spray is a temporary fix only.
- **Action item:** Replace V-belt set during the next service session. Add to the inspect-first list in `parts_to_order.md` — confirm belt P/N with MB-osat (M119 uses multiple V-belts, not a single serpentine).

#### 9. Driver Door — Intermittent Hard-to-Close ⚠️
- The driver door occasionally requires noticeably more force to close than the passenger door.
- **Cause identified:** The driver side window sits slightly too high and contacts the door seal on closing, creating resistance.
- **Action item:** Adjust the driver window maximum height (stop position). The R129 frameless windows have adjustable upper limit stops — either mechanical adjustment at the window regulator or, on some variants, via the door module calibration. Low priority but should be addressed to prevent long-term seal wear.

*   **Touch mapping correct out of the box.** The `autotouch` package (pre-installed on RPi OS with labwc) automatically maps touch coordinates to the rotated display. No manual calibration needed.
*   **Power via touch USB only.** The display has three connectors: HDMI, touch micro-USB, and power micro-USB. The touch USB cable supplies enough current for the AMOLED panel — the dedicated power port is redundant. **Final cabling: 2 cables (HDMI + touch USB).**
*   **USB cable gotcha.** First two micro-USB cables tested were charge-only (no data lines). No USB enumeration at all — `lsusb` showed nothing. Third cable (verified data cable) worked immediately.

*   **Final verified configuration:**
    *   Pi 5 HDMI-0 → display HDMI input (video)
    *   Pi 5 USB-A → display touch micro-USB (touch + power)
    *   Kanshi config: `transform 90` on `HDMI-A-1`
    *   Display: 1920×1080 landscape, touch working, no separate power needed

**Root cause:** Reconnecting the battery with the meter in series exposes it to a massive inrush spike. The correct technique requires a **bypass wire** to avoid this:

**Correct procedure (for next attempt):**
1. Disconnect battery negative cable.
2. Connect a **thick bypass/jumper wire** directly between battery negative post and the ground cable — this carries all current, meter is NOT in circuit.
3. Connect the meter in series **alongside** (in parallel with) the bypass wire.
4. **Remove the bypass wire** — current now flows through the meter at the already-settled level, no inrush surge.
5. Route meter leads out through trunk seal gap, close trunk, lock car with key.
6. Wait 30–45 minutes for all modules to sleep.
7. Read. Acceptable: <50 mA. Typical healthy R129: 20–35 mA.
