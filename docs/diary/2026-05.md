#### Setup

- **Power:** Old Varta H3 100Ah (the previous-generation battery, retired from the car but still showing 12.5 V resting) used as standalone bench source. 40 A AGU fuse holder inline on the +12 V leg, per the permanent-install spec in `work/audio_bench_test.md` §2.

The chassis "Auto Remote" slide switch was originally set to ON (auto-detect via DC offset on high-level inputs), which is the wrong position for bench operation without a head unit. Switched to OFF and power-cycled — only then did the amp respond to the REM pin.

Windows recognized the audio device as `HD-AUDIO USB-INTERFACE FS` immediately without driver installation (FS = Full Speed, up to 96 kHz / 32-bit, class-compliant).
- **Music:** AC/DC + speaker test tracks streamed from the PC at full Windows volume.

#### Gotchas captured (now in `work/audio_bench_test.md` §9.3)

Four traps consumed real time before audio finally flowed. Documenting them in the bench test README so the next reader doesn't re-pay the same tuition:

1. **Save & Store is mandatory.** Every PC-Tool change is preview-only until the disk icon is clicked. Roughly 30 minutes of "I configured everything correctly, why is it silent?" debugging until I tried the explicit save. Single biggest gotcha of the evening.
2. **Auto Remote switch must be OFF for bench operation.** Default position is ON (auto-detect via DC offset), which is wrong with no head unit in the loop. Power-cycle required after flipping — the position is only read at boot.
3. **HEC/AUX Routing is a separate matrix from Main Routing.** Populating Main Routing while DCM has switched the active source to HEC/AUX → silence. Both matrices must be configured if both source paths will eventually be used.
4. **DSP PC-Tool has no internal signal generator.** External audio source mandatory — RTA is analyzer-only, not a tone generator. (Spent a few minutes hunting for a "play test tone" button in the RTA tab that doesn't exist.)

Honourable mentions: the PC-Tool Master Volume slider lives at the bottom edge of the Outputs screen and is easy to miss; channel level meters live on the left/right edges of the same screen and only animate when audio is actively flowing through that channel post-routing.

#### Result

Everything works.

- **Hardware integrity:** UP 6DSP, MEC HD-USB, both Hertz tweeters, both Hertz mid-woofers, Helix sub driver and terminal cup — all confirmed functional.
- **Channel assignment:** L/R correct end-to-end; sub receives summed mono L+R per the DSP mixer.
- **Sub polarity:** cone moves outward first on bass — both DVC2 coils correctly in phase, no need to swap polarity at the terminal cup.
- **Subjective sound quality** on the carpet, with speakers loose and sub in temporary mock-up: "already pretty good on some material." Mid-bass thin (no door reinforcement) and sub a bit boomy and uneven (no proper enclosure or polyfill yet), but the system clearly responds to good source material with character that the BE2210 alone never had. Promising baseline.
- **Configuration persisted** to DSP non-volatile memory via Save & Store; survives power-cycle. Ready to be the starting preset for the in-car tuning pass.

#### Notes for next time

- **The "Save & Store" gotcha is universal across Audiotec Fischer DSPs** (Helix, Match, Brax — same PC-Tool, same behaviour). Worth flagging in any audio docs that involve tuning, not just the bench test README. Future me will hit this again on the first in-car tuning session if not reminded.
- **Bench-testing without a head unit is genuinely a different procedure** from the BE2210 path that the original `audio_bench_test.md` was written for. The §9 variant captures it; the §1–§8 procedure now reads as "what to do during the in-car install" rather than "what to do on the bench." That asymmetry is fine — both are needed at different stages.
- **The MEC HD-USB module pays for itself on the bench alone.** Without it, the only way to bench-test would have been to either (a) move the BE2210 out of the dash, or (b) build a stripped-AUX-cable adapter into the high-level inputs. Both are real work; the MEC card turns "bench test" into "plug in two USB cables and play music." Worth remembering when evaluating its in-car value too — the high-resolution USB input from a phone or laptop is a genuinely useful permanent feature.

### May 2, 2026 (Saturday, evening) -- Pi relocated to rear passenger cubby alongside DSP; consolidated shopping list

Architectural reconsideration of the Pi5 location during the Phase 5 cable planning, prompted by realising the Pi+stack is becoming a collection (Pi5 + NVMe HAT + nRF93M1 cellular modem EVK + Carlinkit CarPlay dongle + future expansion) and the front cubby is the wrong place to service it once the display fascia closes over it.

#### The reconsideration

Original plan: Pi in the front cubby (above the climate control), display in the same cubby. Pi ↔ DSP USB was a ~2 m long pull (C2 + C3 in the manifest).

New plan: **Pi moves to the rear passenger cubby alongside the DSP.** Display stays in the front cubby (where the driver looks).

**Drivers:**
1. **Service access.** Front cubby ends up sealed behind a flush smoked-acrylic fascia per the `UI_rpi5/partslist.md` Option A "99% Stock" aesthetic. Reaching the Pi for any debugging means peeling trim. Rear cubby is reachable from behind the passenger seat without touching the dashboard.
2. **The hottest data path becomes local.** Pi ↔ DSP USB Audio Class collapses from a ~2 m long pull to a ~10 cm in-cubby cable. The single highest-bandwidth digital audio path becomes a non-issue.
3. **Stack growth headroom.** The rear cubby has ~17 L envelope (DSP allocates ~4–5 L with its 40 mm heatsink clearance, Pi stack ~1 L, wiring ~1 L → ~6–7 L of 17 L used). Front cubby is much tighter and is dominated by the display's footprint.
4. **Power consolidation.** Pi takes 12 V from the local DSP rail (Biltema 84-574 8 mm² CCA, 40 A AGU) via a small in-cubby 12 V → 5 V buck. No dedicated Pi power feed forward needed.

**Initial concern that turned out to be a non-issue:** I overestimated the front ↔ rear distance. R129 is a 2-seater so the rear passenger cubby sits immediately behind the passenger seat — ~1–1.25 m to the front cubby, ~2 m budget with comfortable margin for routing on both ends. This is well below the 5 m conservative limit for passive HDMI 1.4 at 1080p, well below USB 2.0's 5 m, and trivial for a TRS shielded audio cable. The HDMI long-run risk that was the main pause-point essentially disappears at this distance.

#### Front cubby attachment finding (recon 2026-05-02)

While the trim was open, confirmed that the original wooden cubby lid attaches to the center console with **4 screws onto a flat installation surface**. This is a clean unblock for the display mounting plan: replace the wooden lid with a rigid mounting plate (3 mm aluminium or 6 mm plywood/MDF, ~140 × 75 mm + screw flange) screwed into those same 4 holes, with the AMOLED panel mounted to the plate via its M2.5 holes. The decorative top finish (smoked glass / acrylic) becomes a separate later task — I can get the system fully functional with a bare plate-borne display, then iterate on the visible fascia at leisure. Decoupling the structural mount from the cosmetic finish removes a pre-existing project blocker.
