#### Setup

Windows recognized the audio device as `HD-AUDIO USB-INTERFACE FS` immediately without driver installation (FS = Full Speed, up to 96 kHz / 32-bit, class-compliant).

#### Gotchas captured (now in `work/audio_bench_test.md` §9.3)

Four traps consumed real time before audio finally flowed. Documenting them in the bench test README so the next reader doesn't re-pay the same tuition:

1. **Save & Store is mandatory.** Every PC-Tool change is preview-only until the disk icon is clicked. Roughly 30 minutes of "I configured everything correctly, why is it silent?" debugging until I tried the explicit save. Single biggest gotcha of the evening.
2. **Auto Remote switch must be OFF for bench operation.** Default position is ON (auto-detect via DC offset), which is wrong with no head unit in the loop. Power-cycle required after flipping — the position is only read at boot.
3. **HEC/AUX Routing is a separate matrix from Main Routing.** Populating Main Routing while DCM has switched the active source to HEC/AUX → silence. Both matrices must be configured if both source paths will eventually be used.
4. **DSP PC-Tool has no internal signal generator.** External audio source mandatory — RTA is analyzer-only, not a tone generator. (Spent a few minutes hunting for a "play test tone" button in the RTA tab that doesn't exist.)

Honourable mentions: the PC-Tool Master Volume slider lives at the bottom edge of the Outputs screen and is easy to miss; channel level meters live on the left/right edges of the same screen and only animate when audio is actively flowing through that channel post-routing.

#### Result

Everything works.

- **Hardware integrity:** UP 6DSP, MEC HD-USB, both Hertz tweeters, both Hertz mid-woofers, Helix sub driver and terminal cup — all confirmed functional.
- **Channel assignment:** L/R correct end-to-end; sub receives summed mono L+R per the DSP mixer.
- **Sub polarity:** cone moves outward first on bass — both DVC2 coils correctly in phase, no need to swap polarity at the terminal cup.
- **Subjective sound quality** on the carpet, with speakers loose and sub in temporary mock-up: "already pretty good on some material." Mid-bass thin (no door reinforcement) and sub a bit boomy and uneven (no proper enclosure or polyfill yet), but the system clearly responds to good source material with character that the BE2210 alone never had. Promising baseline.
- **Configuration persisted** to DSP non-volatile memory via Save & Store; survives power-cycle. Ready to be the starting preset for the in-car tuning pass.

#### Notes for next time

- **The "Save & Store" gotcha is universal across Audiotec Fischer DSPs** (Helix, Match, Brax — same PC-Tool, same behaviour). Worth flagging in any audio docs that involve tuning, not just the bench test README. Future me will hit this again on the first in-car tuning session if not reminded.
- **Bench-testing without a head unit is genuinely a different procedure** from the BE2210 path that the original `audio_bench_test.md` was written for. The §9 variant captures it; the §1–§8 procedure now reads as "what to do during the in-car install" rather than "what to do on the bench." That asymmetry is fine — both are needed at different stages.
- **The MEC HD-USB module pays for itself on the bench alone.** Without it, the only way to bench-test would have been to either (a) move the BE2210 out of the dash, or (b) build a stripped-AUX-cable adapter into the high-level inputs. Both are real work; the MEC card turns "bench test" into "plug in two USB cables and play music." Worth remembering when evaluating its in-car value too — the high-resolution USB input from a phone or laptop is a genuinely useful permanent feature.

### May 2, 2026 (Saturday, evening) -- Pi relocated to rear passenger cubby alongside DSP; consolidated shopping list

Architectural reconsideration of the Pi5 location during the Phase 5 cable planning, prompted by realising the Pi+stack is becoming a collection (Pi5 + NVMe HAT + nRF93M1 cellular modem EVK + Carlinkit CarPlay dongle + future expansion) and the front cubby is the wrong place to service it once the display fascia closes over it.

#### The reconsideration

Original plan: Pi in the front cubby (above the climate control), display in the same cubby. Pi ↔ DSP USB was a ~2 m long pull (C2 + C3 in the manifest).

New plan: **Pi moves to the rear passenger cubby alongside the DSP.** Display stays in the front cubby (where the driver looks).

**Drivers:**
1. **Service access.** Front cubby ends up sealed behind a flush smoked-acrylic fascia per the `UI_rpi5/partslist.md` Option A "99% Stock" aesthetic. Reaching the Pi for any debugging means peeling trim. Rear cubby is reachable from behind the passenger seat without touching the dashboard.
2. **The hottest data path becomes local.** Pi ↔ DSP USB Audio Class collapses from a ~2 m long pull to a ~10 cm in-cubby cable. The single highest-bandwidth digital audio path becomes a non-issue.
3. **Stack growth headroom.** The rear cubby has ~17 L envelope (DSP allocates ~4–5 L with its 40 mm heatsink clearance, Pi stack ~1 L, wiring ~1 L → ~6–7 L of 17 L used). Front cubby is much tighter and is dominated by the display's footprint.
4. **Power consolidation.** Pi takes 12 V from the local DSP rail (Biltema 84-574 8 mm² CCA, 40 A AGU) via a small in-cubby 12 V → 5 V buck. No dedicated Pi power feed forward needed.

**Initial concern that turned out to be a non-issue:** I overestimated the front ↔ rear distance. R129 is a 2-seater so the rear passenger cubby sits immediately behind the passenger seat — ~1–1.25 m to the front cubby, ~2 m budget with comfortable margin for routing on both ends. This is well below the 5 m conservative limit for passive HDMI 1.4 at 1080p, well below USB 2.0's 5 m, and trivial for a TRS shielded audio cable. The HDMI long-run risk that was the main pause-point essentially disappears at this distance.

#### Front cubby attachment finding (recon 2026-05-02)

While the trim was open, confirmed that the original wooden cubby lid attaches to the center console with **4 screws onto a flat installation surface**. This is a clean unblock for the display mounting plan: replace the wooden lid with a rigid mounting plate (3 mm aluminium or 6 mm plywood/MDF, ~140 × 75 mm + screw flange) screwed into those same 4 holes, with the AMOLED panel mounted to the plate via its M2.5 holes. The decorative top finish (smoked glass / acrylic) becomes a separate later task — I can get the system fully functional with a bare plate-borne display, then iterate on the visible fascia at leisure. Decoupling the structural mount from the cosmetic finish removes a pre-existing project blocker.

### May 3 (Sunday evening) — Rear-cubby Pi power locked (85 W lighter charger); LFP-for-DSP detour considered and rejected; battery routing channel found

Two power-architecture decisions resolved this evening, both moves *back* toward the simplest viable design after exploring (and rejecting) more elaborate alternatives.

The user picked up an **85 W cigarette-lighter-style multi-port USB charger** (specific brand TBD — to be logged when receipt is reconciled) that will be wired into the rear-cubby Pi power role. Replaces the more elaborate "3-port 12/24 V → USB module + 40 A relay driven by DSP REM-OUT" architecture sketched in this morning's exchange about multi-output USB power.

**Why the lighter-charger approach is actually fine here** (load arithmetic):

| Device | Typical | Peak |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Pi5 (with NVMe HAT, USB-C PD-fed) | 10 W | 25 W |
| nRF93M1 EVK | ~1 W | 2 W |
| Carlinkit dongle | 2–3 W | 3 W |
| Display back-feed via Pi USB-A → C17 | ~5 W | 7 W |
| Future expansion headroom (1–2 small modules) | 0–5 W | 10 W |
| **Total** | **~20 W typical** | **~45 W peak** |

85 W gives ~2× headroom on peak and ~4× on typical. Plus three architectural wins for free:

1. **Cigarette lighter is on KL15** (`MAIN_12` 8 A switched, `permanent_12v: false`, field-verified 2026-04-07 per `r129_data/data/vehicle.yaml`) — so Pi + everything downstream is **automatically ignition-switched**. No parking-rotted battery, no relay needed, no DSP REM-OUT plumbing. Exactly what we were going to engineer with the relay scheme, but free.

3. **Same wiring topology as §5.6b's planned front Qi tap** — Wago 221-413 into the lighter hot wire, branch wire to where it's needed. Single architecture, two cubbies. When investigating routing for the original 8 mm² CCA battery feed (per the 2026-04-24 plan in `parts_to_order.md` §6B and `work/audio_upgrade_blueprint.md` §1), both candidate routes initially looked painful:

- No big-CCA disassembly

### May 4, 2026 (Monday late evening) -- §5.6b 85 W charger source-side LOCKED: path 3 with UPSTREAM (12 V) high-side gating

Quick follow-up to the evening "TBD with three paths" deferral. After thinking through the multi-load situation, locked the architecture: **path 3 (always-on +12 V from the post-AGU CCA rail), with the cabin-node high-side MOSFET gating the 85 W charger's 12 V input upstream.**

#### Why upstream (12 V) gating, not downstream (5 V) gating

The 85 W charger isn't going to feed only the Pi and the Qi pad — it has spare USB-A and USB-C output ports that are presumably useful for other devices over the lifetime of the install (passenger phone, USB-powered accessories, future expansion). I can't bet on each downstream device having well-behaved standby leakage. Per-load gating would need a switch per load, plus per-load knowledge of what counts as "off" — too many trust assumptions to make confidently.

Upstream gating collapses the entire downstream universe into a single power domain: when the cabin node decides "system off", *everything* on the 85 W charger goes off as one switch action. The MOSFET sits between the post-AGU CCA rail and the charger's 12 V input; it's either passing current or it isn't.

Bonus benefit: when "off", the charger's switching converter isn't running at all, so the only conversion losses to budget are the MOSFET's leakage (sub-µA for a low-Iq automotive logic-level part). The downstream-gating model would have left the charger's converter idling at whatever its quiescent power is.

#### Adding always-on loads later is trivial

If a future consumer specifically *needs* always-on (e.g., an OBD-II logger that wants to record cold-start events, a tracker, etc.), it's just a Wago tap on the post-AGU rail + a small regulator/supply for that load. No architectural pre-commitments needed. Always-on loads bypass the high-side switch entirely — they live on the same rail as the cabin node itself.

This is what makes the upstream-gating model elegant: the *only* always-on loads are the ones I explicitly designate as such (cabin node today, possibly others later). Everything else defaults to switched-on-with-system, no per-load decision tree at install time.

#### Battery-drain budget collapses to ~200 µA

When "system off":
- Cabin node always-on: ≤200 µA (designed for this, Stage 6 budget).
- Cabin-node high-side MOSFET leakage: sub-µA (low-Iq logic-level part).
- 85 W charger: 0 (upstream of its own input — switch is open).
- Pi5, Qi pad, all downstream USB accessories: 0 (no power reaches them).
- **Total: ~200 µA**, ~5 mAh per day, < 0.2 Ah per month.

For comparison, a 70 Ah lead-acid battery's typical self-discharge is ~1–3 % per month = ~0.7–2.1 Ah/month. The cabin node is **three orders of magnitude smaller than the battery's natural self-discharge floor**. The R129's existing parasitic loads (clock, immobilizer if present, ECU memory) likely dominate by a similar factor.

This is the cleanest possible outcome — I literally cannot make this part of the system more parasitic-efficient without removing the cabin node entirely.

#### Cabin-node Stage 6 BOM needs updating

The existing `work/cabin_signal_node/README.md` Stage 6 BOM was sized for downstream gating: MOSFET on the Pi's 5 V, ~5 A worst-case load. Upstream gating means switching ~12 V at up to 7.5 A (the Pi5 PD ≤25 W + Qi 15 W = ~3.3 A typical, but the 85 W charger can theoretically draw ~7 A at 12 V if all USB ports are loaded — sized for the worst case).

Re-spec the MOSFET to ≥10 A continuous at 12 V with low Rds(on) (~30 mΩ class). ### May 5, 2026 (Tuesday, late evening) -- Belt routing correction: AOK912 is single-serpentine, not two-belt

Quick verification of the ordered belts before Saturday's bench session turned up an embarrassing but cheap error: the `A 004 997 05 92` (ContiTech AVX13X950) A/C compressor V-belt that arrived 2026-04-27 alongside the main `A 010 997 99 92` (6PK2523) **does not belong on this car**. Engine 119.960 in chassis 129.066 uses a **single serpentine belt** that drives the A/C compressor along with all other accessories — there is no separate A/C V-belt loop.

#### How the error came to light

The owner's superficial visual check at the front of the engine showed only one belt. Initial response was to dismiss this as a viewing-angle issue (the A/C V-belt would normally sit on the front-most plane of the crank pulley, hidden behind the fan/shroud from above). The owner then surfaced the **MB WIS belt-routing diagram** for engine 119.960, model 129.066:

> *"Engine 119.960, model 129.066 / Running diagram with air pump / Belt length 2523 mm, 6 grooves"*

Pulleys labelled in the diagram:
1. Tensioning pulley
2. Crankshaft
3. **AC compressor**
4. Bottom guide pulley
5. Fan
7. Air pump
8. Alternator
10. Power steering pump
11. Coolant pump

A single belt path threads through all of them. The A/C compressor (#3) is on the same loop as everything else. There is no second belt in the diagram. The 2523 mm / 6 grooves spec at the top corresponds exactly to the `A 010 997 99 92` (ContiTech 6PK2523) that is already on hand.

#### Where the original mistake came from

The 2026-04-20 part-number verification (logged in `docs/parts_to_order.md` Priority 1B) cited "SL Shop, Pelican, mbpartsgiant, and benzworld / 500Eboard technical archives" as cross-references. Those forum sources mention a separate A/C V-belt on *some* M-class V8s — but that pattern applies to the earlier M117/M116 V8s and to certain non-129.066 M119 sub-variants, **not** to the M119.960 in the R129 chassis. The forum cross-references conflated engine families and the verification step did not catch it because the WIS diagram was never pulled directly. Owner's quick eyeball ("only one belt visible") was the trigger that surfaced the mismatch.

#### Disposition

- **`A 010 997 99 92` (ContiTech 6PK2523)** — correct, fits, will be installed Saturday 2026-05-09/10 alongside the engine/trans mount swap as planned.
- **`A 004 997 05 92` (ContiTech AVX13X950, 12 × 960 V-belt)** — **kept on the shelf as a generic-spec spare**. Cost was €10.49; the Autodoc RMA round-trip (return shipping, refund processing, restocking risk) is not worth the recovery, and 12 × 960 is a common-enough V-belt size that it might earn its keep on something else in the fleet eventually. If not, the loss is acceptable and pre-paid as the cost of this lesson.
- **MB-osat talking points** in `parts_to_order.md` Priority 1B updated: original talking-point #1 ("confirm A 010 997 99 92 and A 004 997 05 92 by VIN, order both") struck through; no new belt order needed since the correct main belt is already on hand. Tensioner rod (`A 119 200 00 36`) stock-check stays as the only outstanding belt-related ask of MB-osat.

#### Lesson logged

**Pull the WIS belt-routing diagram for the specific engine + chassis combo before ordering. Forum cross-references are not a substitute for the factory diagram, especially across V8 families where pulley counts and accessory layouts differ between sub-variants on otherwise similar engines.** Cheap tuition this time (€10.49) — it would have stung a lot more on a wrong tensioner or wrong pulley set.

The Priority 1B header in `parts_to_order.md` now carries an explicit "Routing correction (2026-05-05 late)" callout so this mistake stays visible as part of the audit trail rather than being silently rewritten out of history.

#### Infinite-baffle vs. sealed enclosure — main question answered

**Question:** Does the new MP 165P.3 need a closed enclosure behind it (built into the small space between the front plate and the door's inner steel face), or is it sufficient to treat the door cavity acoustically and let the woofer fire its rear wave into the cavity?

**Answer: No closed enclosure. Use the door cavity as the rear chamber (infinite-baffle / free-air mount) — but the door must be properly acoustically prepared.**

Reasoning:

1. **The MP 165P.3 is designed for IB / door-cavity mounting.** Hertz's spec sheet for this driver targets free-air or large-volume sealed installations. Vas is in the ~25–30 L range typical for a 6.5" car woofer of this class. The R129 door cavity is roughly 12–18 L of effective volume — large compared to any small box you could fit behind the front plate (a few hundred mL at best), and large enough that it doesn't meaningfully raise Fs or reduce LF extension when used as the rear chamber.
2. **A small sealed box behind the front plate would actively hurt performance.** A 0.5–1 L sealed volume behind the magnet would push the system Fs from the driver's natural ~70 Hz up into the 150–200 Hz range, kill any LF output below the 80 Hz crossover (the very band the woofer is supposed to cover from 80 Hz up to the 2.5 kHz tweeter handoff), and trap heat behind the magnet — none of which are what the driver wants.
3. **Acoustic short-circuit risk is the real concern, not enclosure size.** With no rear enclosure, the woofer's rear wave loops through the door cavity. If the **inner door panel** (the metal sheet between cabin and door cavity, behind the door card and vapor barrier) has unsealed service holes, the rear wave leaks back into the cabin and **cancels the front wave at LF** (acoustic short-circuit; can lose 6–10 dB below ~200 Hz). This is the failure mode that bad door installs suffer from, and it's mistakenly diagnosed as "needs an enclosure" — when the real fix is to seal the inner panel so the door cavity itself becomes the enclosure.

**What "treat the door cavity acoustically" actually means in practice — four-layer treatment:**

| Layer | Where | Purpose | Material |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. **Inner-panel seal** | The door's inner steel sheet (between door card and cavity) — every service hole, harness pass-through, and large opening | Convert the door cavity into a sealed rear chamber for the speaker. Without this, the rear wave short-circuits to the cabin. | Aluminum-faced butyl patches (Stinger Roadkill, generic CLD sheet) sized to cover each hole + 25 mm overlap. **Do NOT seal the small drain holes at the bottom of the door** — those are for water management and small enough to be acoustically negligible. |
| 2. **Outer-skin damping (CLD)** | Outer steel skin (the painted exterior surface, from inside the cavity) | Kill panel resonance modes — stops the door panel ringing and adding distortion / boom around 100–300 Hz. | Constrained-layer butyl-foil (Dynamat, Stinger, Skinz, generic). **25–50 % coverage, not 100 %** — placed at vibration antinodes (panel center, around the speaker, around the window track). 100 % coverage adds weight without proportional benefit. |
| 3. **Outer-skin absorber (CCF / melamine)** | Inside surface of the outer skin, opposite the speaker | Absorb the rear wave so it doesn't reflect off the outer skin and slap back through the cone (which would smear the front wave). | Closed-cell foam (CCF, ~6–10 mm) or melamine acoustic foam, applied directly behind the speaker location on the outer-skin interior. |
| 4. **Speaker basket gasket / decoupler** | Between speaker basket and the front plate / door's inner face | Airtight seal at the basket-to-door joint (mandatory for the inner-panel seal to work) + mechanical decoupling so the basket doesn't vibrate the panel. | CCF gasket strip (3–6 mm closed-cell) around the basket flange perimeter; nyloc nuts + flat washers on the three door bolts to compress the gasket evenly. |

**This is the standard "BIG 3" car-audio door-treatment recipe** (CLD on outer skin + sealed inner panel + CCF absorber), and it's what the MP 165P.3 wants. No purpose-built enclosure.

**Bonus benefit:** the vapor-barrier replacement (punch-list item #2) becomes part of the inner-panel seal. A 0.15 mm PE sheet sealed to the inner face with butyl rope contributes ~3–6 dB of additional inner-panel acoustic isolation on top of the butyl-faced patches over the service holes. #### Tweeter mount — plate material, adhesive, aim

- **Plate material:** 3 mm ABS or PETG sheet works (light, easy to cut, doesn't ring); alternatively a 2 mm aluminum sheet (slightly more inert, sub-resonance well below the 2.5 kHz crossover so non-issue acoustically). **Avoid thin plastics under 2 mm** — they'll vibrate in sympathy with the tweeter dome and add panel coloration. The original factory housing's mounting flange can probably be re-purposed as the plate substrate if it's flat enough.
- **Adhesive:** **3M VHB tape** (4910 / 5952 / similar) is the right choice — used by automotive OEMs for permanent dashboard / trim mounts, vibration-resistant, holds at dashboard temperatures (rated to ~90 °C continuous, ~150 °C peak — comfortably above R129 dash-top temps even in summer). Two-sided foam tape from a hardware store is **not** equivalent — the foam carrier creeps in heat and the bond degrades over a few summers. Avoid hot-melt glue (creeps in heat), avoid cyanoacrylate (brittle, cracks under vibration), avoid two-part epoxy (works but is permanent and removes the option of re-aiming later).
- **Aim:** classic two valid approaches — (a) **on-axis at driver's headrest** (best stereo image from driver's seat, narrower sweet spot, slightly bright on axis), or (b) **cross-firing to opposite-seat headrest** (each L tweeter aims at passenger headrest, R tweeter aims at driver headrest — uses time-intensity trading to widen and center the stage, more forgiving for both seats, slight loss of pinpoint imaging on either side). **Recommendation: cross-firing at ~30° off-axis** — DSP time-alignment will compensate the path-length asymmetry afterward, and cross-firing is the more useful default for a car that gets driven with passengers. Either way, fine-tune in the Phase 4 DSP tuning pass; the aim doesn't need to be perfect at install time because the DSP can re-EQ small mismatches.
- **Clearance:** ensure the dome face has ≥5 mm clearance from the dashboard grille / windscreen / surrounding trim. The MP 28.3 is a low-excursion device (it's a tweeter) but the clearance is more for acoustic decoupling — a dome that's nearly touching a grille modulates above ~5 kHz and adds buzz.

#### Factory wire vs. fresh wire — decision

The user's question on tonight's findings: keep digging through the dashboard / center console to find the factory tweeter filter (so the factory wire path can be re-used, with the filter bypassed/removed), or run a fresh tweeter wire end-to-end from the DSP and ignore the factory wire entirely?

**Decision: run fresh wire end-to-end. Stop hunting for the factory filter.**

Rationale:

1. **The factory filter has to be bypassed anyway, even if found.** The UP 6DSP is providing the active high-pass crossover at ~2.5 kHz (per blueprint Phase 4). Any factory passive filter in series with the tweeter would (a) add an unwanted second-order rolloff that distorts the DSP's intended slope, (b) be sized for the factory tweeter's impedance / sensitivity rather than the MP 28.3's (4 Ω, 91 dB), and (c) probably contain a 30+-year-old electrolytic or bipolar capacitor whose value has drifted. So even if the filter is located, it must be removed or bypassed — there's no scenario where it stays in the signal path.
2. **The factory wire path is also wrong for the new install.** Factory tweeter wire runs from the head unit / filter location to the tweeter mount — fine for a head-unit-driven factory tweeter, but the new path is **DSP (rear passenger cubby) → tweeter (driver-side dash)**, which doesn't share routing with the factory wire. Re-using the factory wire would require splicing at both ends, which is its own connector reliability problem and saves at most ~1.5 m of wire.
3. **The blueprint already specifies fresh tweeter wire** (`work/audio_upgrade_blueprint.md` Phase 2 step 3). Tonight's findings reinforce that the blueprint plan is correct — there's no actual benefit to re-using the factory wire, only the cost of finding and bypassing the filter.
4. **Finding the filter would burn 1–2 hours** of further center-console / dashboard disassembly with no payoff. That time is better spent on the door treatment, the fresh wire pull, or any other in-flight item.

The factory tweeter wire and filter can stay where they are, disconnected at the speaker end. If a future restoration ever wants to return to factory configuration, the original wiring is intact and the filter is wherever it always was.

### May 12, 2026 (Tuesday) -- DSP6 now has one real in-car tune: soft top up, driver-head position

Tonight moved the audio system from "promising and partly provisional" to a genuinely tuned configuration, but only for one clearly defined use case:

**soft top up, measurement centred around the driver's head.**

That caveat matters. This is not yet a top-down tune, not a hard-top tune, and not a balanced driver/passenger compromise. The current preset is intentionally selfish: make the system image and sum correctly at the driver's seat with the soft top on.

#### Fully active front stage established

The factory passive crossover path is now bypassed for tuning purposes. Temporary direct wiring put each front driver on its own UP 6DSP channel:

| DSP channel role | Driver |
| :--- | :--- |
| Left tweeter | Hertz MP 28.3 |
| Right tweeter | Hertz MP 28.3 |
| Left midbass | Hertz MP 165P.3 |
| Right midbass | Hertz MP 165P.3 |

That was the unlock. Until this point, the right tweeter's factory passive HP filter kept one side electrically different from the other, so any serious EQ or imaging work would have been tuning around a temporary defect. With all four front drivers individually addressable, the DSP could finally do what it was bought for: crossover, level, delay, phase, and EQ as one system.

The REW routing problem from the first sessions also got resolved. Independent left/right measurement is now reliable enough that channel-specific sweeps can be trusted instead of guessed from mixed output behaviour.

#### Crossover, phase, and time alignment

The biggest acoustic fault was a severe cancellation around **100 Hz**. The subwoofer and door midbass were arriving effectively out of phase through the crossover region, so the system was making bass energy and then deleting a large part of it acoustically at the listening position.

Independent phase inversions in the DSP fixed the summation. After that, the rear 10" sealed sub and the front midbass drivers started acting like one low-frequency system instead of separate boxes fighting each other.

Speaker distances were then mapped into the Match DSP delay model. This was the second major step: once the rear sub was time-aligned to the front stage, it stopped sounding like a source behind the seats. Bass now arrives from the front image with the rest of the music. The sub is still physically in the rear cubby, but psychoacoustically it no longer announces itself there.

#### Spatial averaging and EQ

REW spatial averages were captured for the left and right channels around the driver-head area rather than trusting a single fixed microphone point. The left and right EQs were then treated independently.

The important result: the previous **~10 dB left/right midrange mismatch** could be pulled into overlap. That did more than make the graph prettier. It locked the center image. Vocals and snare now sit in the middle instead of wandering with frequency, and the soundstage detaches from the visible speaker positions in a way I did not expect from this cabin.

For the top end, I avoided a hard digital shelf. Instead, the high-frequency shaping uses a **15 kHz low-pass at 12 dB/octave**. That behaves more like natural absorption: enough harmonic air remains, but the glass/trim glare is kept under control.

#### Result

This beat the expectation I had when I started the audio upgrade.

**Three independent analyses converged on the same verdict**:

1. **Order-axis PSD comparison** (`work/exhaust_2026_05_14/order_axis_compare.png`):
   plotting the spectrum with x-axis = `frequency / firing_rate` for both the
   rev-up region (30-37 s) and the plateau (38-50 s) in m2. Clean peaks at
   integer firing orders 1× and 2×, **no peaks at half-integer orders
   (1.5×, 2.5×, 3.5×) above noise floor**. Half-integer firing harmonics
   would be the signature of paired-cylinder asymmetry; they're absent.

2. **Low-frequency (0-35 Hz) waterfall** (`low_freq_waterfall.png`):
   shows a clear bright trace climbing **from ~12 Hz at idle to ~33 Hz at the
   rev plateau** and dying the instant the engine stops at 64 s. Frequency
   tracks `rpm/60` exactly = **1× engine-rotation speed = engine block
   rocking on its mounts**. This is exactly the visible idle shake the
   driver has been observing for months. Largely infrasonic so not directly
   audible, but the UMIK-1 catches it. **Worn engine + transmission mounts
   are the most likely cause** on a 35-year-old car. Engine combustion is
   not the source.

3. **Firing-line envelope modulation** (`firing_envelope_modulation.png`):
   bandpass filtering the firing line (40-60 Hz) at idle and taking its
   Hilbert envelope reveals a sharp peak at **6.38 Hz** at idle — which is
   exactly the cylinder-event rate (`fire/8 = 51 Hz / 8 = 6.4 Hz at this
   RPM`). This is **not a Welch bin-spacing artifact** (8 s window has
   0.125 Hz resolution, the line is real). The original GoPro "6.1 Hz
   sideband" was almost certainly the same feature, just rounded by
   coarser bin spacing. Interpretation: `fire ± 1×cyl_rate` modulation
   represents *non-uniform per-cylinder cycle-to-cycle variation*, which
   combined with the absence of half-integer harmonics is the signature
   of normal cycle-to-cycle variability + possibly a few-percent
   single-cylinder offset — well within healthy aged-engine territory.

**Bottom line: healthy V8.** No misfire signature, no cylinder-pair
asymmetry, normal cycle-to-cycle variability. The dominant 40-70 Hz
"fixed band" in the spectrum is acoustic resonance of the cabin / exhaust
pipe (confirmed by RPM independence), **not** an engine feature. The
visible idle shake is mechanical (mounts), not combustion.

#### Addendum (May 14 afternoon) — click bug fixed; sport preset tuned against M119 reference

"step responses including high-freq content, repeating at 5–20 Hz." Diagnosed
via first-difference + envelope-spectrum analysis on `gate0_oem_idle.wav`
through `gate0_sport_idle.wav` — clear envelope peaks at exactly 6 Hz (cylinder-
event rate) and 12 Hz (1× engine speed) at idle. Root cause: the 8-element
cylinder amplitude/phase signature was indexed with `floor(firing_count) % 8`,
producing a piecewise-constant modulator that step-changes at every firing
transition (~48 Hz). Multiplied by the continuous tonal sine, each step is a
click with all harmonics; the 8-cylinder pattern repeats once per engine
cycle so the loudest steps recur at 6 Hz. Fix: linear interpolation between
the 8 entries on continuous `firing_count`, making the modulator piecewise-
linear (1/k² harmonic rolloff instead of 1/k → silent at audible levels).
First-difference RMS dropped from −66 dB to −73 dB on OEM; the visible
6/12 Hz envelope peaks dropped 3+ dB and the time-domain spike pattern is
gone. Same fix preserves the cylinder-rate sideband structure at the desired

#### Addendum 2 (May 14 evening) — residual clicks were not a "click bug", they were three independent over-excited modulation paths

User reported the v3.2 fix did *not* remove the perceived click pattern.
Visual inspection of `click_diagnosis_post.png` confirmed: rows of
amplitude-modulated impulses still visible. The first-difference RMS
metric I'd been using had improved, but only because the linear-interp
fix addressed *one* of three independent click sources. The other two
were over-excited modulation knobs that I'd dialed by ear without
checking against the m1 measured target.

**Diagnosis (gate0 idle, 720 rpm, 500 Hz HP-filter envelope spectrum):**

| Source                              | Mechanism                                                                                                | OEM/Lux symptom            | Sport symptom                |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **A**. Cyl-signature step (v3.2 fix) | piecewise-constant 8-element cylinder amp/phase table → step at every firing                              | Fixed (linear interp)      | Fixed (linear interp)        |
| **B**. `cyl_amp_spread` over-excited | 0.10–0.20 spread produced 20+% per-pulse amplitude variation; real engine = 0.6 % envelope @ 6 Hz (m1 measured) | Hidden by low pulse_gain   | Strongly audible 6 / 12 Hz pattern |
| **C**. `pulse_jitter` over-excited   | 0.04–0.20 → ±10–20% per-firing amplitude variation; real CCV is ~1%                                       | Visible noise floor pattern | Pulse-CV 0.20                |
| **D**. Pure-sine half-integer harmonics | `sin(1.5·firing_phase)` and `sin(2.5·firing_phase)` perfectly phase-locked to fundamental → beat at exactly 0.5×fire = 24 Hz | (n/a — these are zero on OEM/Lux) | Dominant 24 Hz envelope line, "loud-quiet-loud-quiet" pulse pattern |

**Fixes (v3.5):**

1. `cyl_amp_spread` reduced 5–7×: OEM 0.10→0.015, luxury 0.18→0.020,
   amg 0.16→0.025, sport 0.20→0.030. Tuned to match m1's measured
   −45 dB envelope-spectrum line at 6 Hz (≈0.6 % depth).
2. `bank_asymmetry` reduced similarly.
3. `pulse_jitter` reduced 5–10× to engine-realistic ~1 % CCV.
4. **New: phase-noise on half-integer harmonics.** Real engines'
   1.5×fire and 2.5×fire harmonics are not phase-coherent with the
   fundamental — they emerge from bank-firing alternation +
   manifold/exhaust resonance, both of which jitter cycle-to-cycle and
   broaden the spectral lines to ~5 Hz wide. Implemented in
   `v8_synth.py` as a per-block leaky OU phase walk
   (`_half_int_phase[2]`, ~3 Hz BW, stationary RMS 1.5 rad), ramped
   smoothly across the block. Matches the real spectrum's *broadened*
   peaks instead of pure tones, and breaks the rigid 24 Hz beat.

**Quantitative result on gate0 sport idle (720 rpm):**

| Metric                                   | v3.2 baseline | v3.5 (after) | Δ        |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------ | -------- |
| Pulse-amplitude CV (96 firings, 2 s)     | ~0.20         | 0.095        | −53 %    |
| 4–30 Hz HP-envelope peak                 | −55 dB        | −68.7 dB     | −13.7 dB |
| Loudest sub-firing-rate envelope freq    | 24 Hz @ −55 dB | 13.5 Hz @ −68.7 dB | broadened, halved |

OEM and Luxury idle now show:
- Pulse-CV 0.023 / 0.026 (= 2.3 / 2.6 %, matches real-engine CCV)
- 4–30 Hz HP-env peak below −108 dB (essentially inaudible)

producing the same audible symptom by independent paths. Only when all
three were addressed simultaneously did the audible pattern collapse.
Lesson for the workflow: tune modulation knobs against measured envelope
depth at known frequencies, not by ear.

#### nRF54L15 → Pi link moves from USB-CDC to UART

Decision: drop the planned USB-CDC link and use a UART instead.

Drivers:

1. **Pi5 USB-A port budget.** The Pi has 4 × USB-A; with USB-CDC
   nRF54L15 the budget was 4-of-4 used (DSP audio sink, Carlinkit
   CarPlay, nRF93M1 RNDIS+AT, nRF54L15 CDC), zero spare. With UART
   for the L15, 3-of-4 used and one spare for diagnostic console /
   future joystick / emergency rescue. **The hub planned in the
   original BOM (R7 line) becomes optional, not required.**
2. **The wire format already supports it.** `R129/FW_nrf/payload/`
   is a portable C library that's transport-agnostic — same framed
   payload codec runs over USB-CDC, UART, or BLE notify. M0/M1 in
   the FW_nrf README was already verified on UART0. Switching the
   production link to UART is a Zephyr DTS / Kconfig change, not a
   refactor.
3. **Voltage-level compatibility.** Both nRF54L15 GPIO and Pi5 GPIO
   are 3.3 V. No level shifter. Three wires: nRF TX → Pi RX, Pi TX
   → nRF RX, common ground.
4. **Data rate is trivial.** BLE proximity heartbeats + presence
   frames + shutdown handshake at 1 Hz is ~50 bytes/sec. 115200
   baud has 99 % headroom; 9600 would suffice.

Pi-side configuration: enable the PL011 UART by adding `enable_uart=1`
in `/boot/firmware/config.txt`. To preserve Bluetooth (which shares
the default UART by default), use `dtoverlay=uart2` (or 3/4/5 — Pi5
has 5 UARTs available via GPIO header) instead of disabling BT. The
nRF54L15 side: `chosen { zephyr,console = &uart0; ... }` already
defaults to the PCA10156 P0.20/P0.22 pins, no DTS work needed.

#### Manual 50 A power switch + ATO fuse box — promoted to permanent design element

Originally conceived as an interim ("until the always-on cabin node
firmware is mature"), the manual hard-kill switch is now locked in as
**permanent infrastructure**. The reasoning shift is:

- Every production embedded-vehicle ECU has a transport-mode /
  service-disable hard kill that drops to **zero firmware-dependent
  parasitic drain**. This is good engineering hygiene, not a
  scaffold.
- The IRF4905 high-side MOSFET in the cabin signal node (Stage 6b
  of `work/cabin_signal_node/README.md`) is the **firmware-driven**
  on/off path. The 50 A rocker switch in series with it is the
  **firmware-independent** path. Both can interrupt the +12 V to
  the 85 W charger; either one off → charger off → Pi off.
- Use cases for the manual switch over the entire system lifetime:
  service disable while plate is on bench, firmware-bug recovery if
  the MOSFET ever latches in a bad state, long-storage
  zero-parasitic-drain mode, diagnostic forcing-on regardless of
  phone presence.

Architecture in series, fuse closest to the +12 V source per
automotive convention (factory upstream lighter-circuit fuse 8 A,
backstop):

```
+12 V from cubby wiring → ATO fuse (7.5 A slow-blow on the charger
                          branch; spare slots reserved for cabin-node
                          always-on rail @ 1–2 A fast-blow when added)
                       → 50 A rocker switch (manual hard-kill,
                          permanent control)
                       → 85 W cigarette-lighter USB charger
                       → Pi5 USB-C 5 V + Carlinkit + nRF93M1 + nRF54L15
                          (latter two via UART — see above; only
                          their 5 V power comes through the charger)
```

Switch is rated 50 A continuous (overkill by ~5–7× for the ~7 A
charger draw, which means contact erosion is essentially zero over
the install lifetime). Two-terminal, no integrated illumination —
"ON" label orientation will be verified with a continuity meter
before final wiring (cheap rocker switches are sometimes mis-labeled
relative to internal contact state).

Fuse-rating logic: 85 W ÷ 12 V ≈ 7.1 A out, ÷ 0.85 efficiency ≈ 8.4 A
peak in. 7.5 A slow-blow (blade ATO) handles the charger's
transient inrush without false-tripping while still being tightly
selective vs. the upstream factory 8 A (any local fault opens the
local fuse first).

#### Cooling architecture — lid stays uncut, foam-baffled side-trim vents

Re-derived from first principles after the user's "lid off → exhaust
drone audible" observation:

| Constraint | Implication |
| :--- | :--- |
| OEM lid is acoustic insulation (verified by listening) | **No air paths through the lid.** All intake/exhaust must go through cubby trim side walls (replaceable plastic) or the rear bulkhead (already has a fish-string pass-through to trunk). |
| Heat budget ~25 W steady, ~50 W peak | Air-cooled is sufficient. Liquid is overkill (~80–100 W threshold). |
| ~2.5 K rise across cubby at 20 CFM airflow | Even a 40 mm 5 V fan handles it; a 60 mm 12 V fan at moderate RPM is comfortable margin. |

Architecture:

- **Intake:** small (~50 mm) hole in the cubby trim **side wall**,
  low position, hidden behind passenger-seat back / carpet.
  Foam-baffled plenum on the cabin side (open-cell PU foam,
  ~30–50 mm depth) replaces the lid's acoustic seal — kills
  high-frequency noise (electronics whine, fan blade noise,
  exhaust drone) while passing cooling air freely. Standard
  rack-cabinet noise-control technique.
- **Fan:** 60 mm 12 V brushless case fan (Arctic F6 12 V or Noctua
  NF-A6x25 + 12 V→5 V buck), inline with intake, blowing air INTO
  the cubby. Rubber-grommet mount, not rigid — keeps fan vibration
  out of the structural NVH path. Wired to DSP REM line (runs only
  with audio system on) per original BOM line R7.
- **Exhaust:** passive, two options pending the bulkhead pass-through
  geometry verification:
  1. **Opposite side wall, foam-baffled** (mirror of intake) —
     simpler, in-cabin cross-flow, dumps heat back into the cabin.
  2. **Through the rear bulkhead into the trunk via the May 3
     fish-string pass-through** — acoustically superior (no return
     path into cabin) and dumps heat into a less-conditioned space.
     The fish-string's existing path is the irreversible step; this
     reuses it for an exhaust tube instead of (or alongside) the
     planned LTE coax.

Lid stays untouched.

### May 23, 2026 (Saturday) -- Post-MB-osat drivability: three observations within 24 h reshape the engine-mount priority and open a focused throttle/vacuum/trans-modulator follow-up

First full day of driving after the 2026-05-22 MB-osat handover. Three observations, two new symptoms and one positive baseline shift, all of which appear to share a single root-cause class — something in the throttle / vacuum / trans-modulator area changed during the MB-osat engine-bay work — and which together meaningfully reshape what comes next.

Full diagnostic ladder + working hypothesis + spec-vs-observed table are in the new work item: [`work/post_mbosat_drivability/README.md`](../../work/post_mbosat_drivability/README.md). Diary captures the high-level findings and the knock-on effects on other open work items.

#### The three observations

1. **Warm idle is higher than May 22 captured.** Steady at **800–900 rpm in P/N** against a 600–700 rpm spec for M119.960 / 722.3 — about 150–250 rpm above target, not the "~50–100 rpm" the drive-home note recorded yesterday. Idle is stable (not hunting), just at the wrong target. Either the condition has worsened over the first heat cycle or the initial impression understated it; either is possible.
2. **Kickdown "glitching" on 2 of 4 WOT acceleration events today.** Rapid downshift → immediate upshift sequence at or just after pedal-down on the affected events, instead of the clean kickdown-and-hold the 722.3 used to produce; other 2 of 4 events kicked down cleanly. New, intermittent symptom. Self-acknowledged owner caveat: small sample and operator-pedal-modulation variance is plausible — a 2/4 split is *exactly* what intermittent pedal-rate variance would produce. Two other candidate mechanisms also fit a 2/4 hit rate: marginal vacuum-modulator signal (loose hose dropping out under transient manifold-vacuum swings) or borderline trans line pressure (modulator-fed pressure offset). Not yet conclusive — owner agreed to capture a larger sample (~10 WOT events on varied routes, per-event notes on clean/glitch + gear + RPM + temp) before treating as a confirmed fault. Diagnostic priority unchanged: Step 4 of the work-item ladder still addresses two of the three mechanisms.
3. **Visible engine vibration at low rpm is gone, identical in P, N, D, and R.** Positive finding, and the most diagnostically interesting of the three. The **P/N-vs-D/R independence is the key**: P/N has the engine spinning the torque-converter shell under no load, D/R loads the engine via the converter against the brake. If vibration source were mounts, exhaust hangers, or driveline geometry, the load state would change the amplitude. It doesn't. That points squarely at an **engine-combustion-side** source that is no longer happening — i.e. the loose vacuum hoses MB-osat reconnected yesterday were causing cylinder-to-cylinder firing variance, and with that fixed, the engine is now firing evenly enough to be visibly smooth in all four selector positions.

#### Working hypothesis — one root-cause class for both Obs 1 and Obs 2

The MB-osat engine-bay walkthrough on May 22 touched three things in the throttle / vacuum area: (a) reconnected "a couple of loose vacuum hoses", (b) ran a smoke test, (c) applied silicone tape to the cracked 5th-cyl injector breather. Any of those touch-points could have left a downstream artifact.

The single best-fit mechanism that explains both Obs 1 and Obs 2 with one cause is a **preloaded throttle linkage** — cruise rod, kickdown cable, or accelerator cable not re-seated cleanly after the hose work, lifting the throttle plate slightly off its idle stop. The cruise actuator, kickdown cable, and accelerator cable all attach to a single throttle-linkage cluster right at the throttle body — where MB-osat had to work to reach the loose vacuum hoses. One nudged rod or cable preload would produce both symptoms (high idle from uncompensable air past the partially-open plate; kickdown shift logic confused because the kickdown cable position vs throttle position is now wrong).

Second-best fit: **trans vacuum modulator line misseated** (would explain Obs 2 but not Obs 1 directly). Co-suspect, addressed by Step 4 of the work-item ladder. Third candidates: a different vacuum hose loose since the visit, or a sticky/contaminated LLR exposed now that the chronic leaks aren't masking it. All four hypotheses fall out of the same diagnostic ladder.

#### Knock-on effect on the engine-mount priority — downshifted

The May 22 diary and the `known_issues.md` "Engine Mounts" entry both elevated the mount swap to "the next highest-yield diagnostic for residual cabin vibration", with a recommended pre-swap UMIK-1 capture to enable quantitative A/B against the May 14 data. **Obs 3 today substantially weakens that case.** If the vibration is gone *because firing is even now*, the mounts were probably never the dominant contributor. They were transmitting the firing-variance shake, not generating it.

The Corteco mounts (×3, on hand since 2026-03-30) are still 35 years old and still worth installing eventually as preventive maintenance, but **the diagnostic-driven urgency has evaporated.** Priority drops from MEDIUM to LOW in `known_issues.md`. Plan changes from "book a dedicated Saturday in early June with pre/post UMIK-1" to "fold into the next opportunistic front-belly-pan-off session" (brake flex hose inspection, oil change with magnetic plug install, whatever comes next that already needs the front belly pan off).

#### Knock-on effect on the May 14 acoustic-signature interpretation

`work/audio_exhaust_synth/m119_sideband_diagnosis.md` interpreted the clean 1×-rev infrasonic line as engine-block rocking on (likely worn) mounts. With Obs 3, the simpler explanation is **cylinder-to-cylinder firing variance from the loose vacuum hoses** — small unmetered-air leaks bias individual cylinders lean by different amounts each combustion cycle, producing a 1×-rev variability signature that *looks* like rocking but is really irregular firing energy. The line was real, the mathematics held — only the physical interpretation needs a footnote / addendum: post-2026-05-22 vacuum-hose fix, a UMIK-1 re-capture would show the line substantially attenuated if this interpretation is correct. (Worth running once the post-MB-osat-drivability work-item Step 1 is closed and the warm idle is back at spec — both the engine and the measurement environment will be in a cleaner state.)

This shifts the M119 acoustic-signature verdict from "healthy V8 with worn mounts" to **"healthy V8 (verbatim), period."** A satisfying tightening of the diagnostic story.

### May 25, 2026 (Monday) -- Post-MB-osat drivability follow-up: kickdown "glitch" retracted as misread of normal 722.3 behaviour; throttle-linkage hypothesis weakened by partial Step 1; vacuum-hose sweep + LLR test now the two remaining diagnostics

Short progress entry on the drivability follow-up started two days ago. Two of the three open items moved — one closed cleanly, one weakened — and the work-item ladder narrows to two cheap tests.

#### Kickdown "glitch" retracted (Obs 2 → resolved as normal operation)

Owner ran controlled-condition WOT tests on today's drive. Two diagnostic windows:

- WOT launched from a low-enough speed that 1st gear would correctly engage → **clean run to redline**.
- WOT launched above the 1st-gear range (i.e. kickdown 4→3 or 4→2 only, never to 1st) → **clean run to redline**.

The 2-of-4 events that triggered the "glitch" impression on May 23 were almost certainly **WOT punches at the edge of the 1st-gear engagement zone**, where the 722.3 correctly drops to 1st for a few hundred milliseconds and then upshifts to 2nd as engine RPM crosses the 1→2 shift point. That short 1st→2nd sequence feels like "a very brief boost then a step-back" — exactly the "glitch" sensation the owner reported — but it is **factory-correct kickdown behaviour** for a 4-speed automatic with relatively tall gearing at low road speed. The owner's original "this might be due to my actions" caveat was correct; the misread came from inconsistent starting-speed across the four test events rather than inconsistent trans behaviour.

### May 27, 2026 (Wednesday evening) — CarPlay re-pair procedure pinned down; UI sidebar polarity inverted; long-press exit added

In-car shakedown session of the R129 UI in the car. Two related goals: (1) sort out a chronic CarPlay reconnect failure that had been preventing reliable use, and (2) ship the daylight-visibility sidebar changes drafted earlier in the week. Both closed out tonight.

#### Background — what had been failing

Symptom pattern over the last few weeks:
- LIVI launches cleanly when the CarPlay sidebar slot is tapped.
- First connection of the day usually works.
- After leaving CarPlay running for an hour, or after switching to other UI screens and back, the dongle frequently fails to reconnect to the phone — LIVI shows a small slowly-blinking square (its "waiting for handshake" indicator), the phone shows no CarPlay UI, and even a Pi reboot doesn't always fix it.
- No way to exit CarPlay from the UI: the LIVI window is opaque, the sidebar is hidden behind it once focused, and there was no gesture or button to stop the LIVI subprocess.

#### Fix 1 — long-press on the CarPlay sidebar slot to stop LIVI

Smallest possible UI change to make the system recoverable without SSH or reboot. Implementation in `UI_rpi5/src/sidebar.py` + `UI_rpi5/src/main_window.py`:

- New `carplay_stop_requested` PyQt5 signal on the `Sidebar` widget.
- `mousePressEvent` for the CarPlay slot now starts a 600 ms `QTimer` instead of activating the slot immediately. Other slots keep their existing tap-to-activate behaviour.
- `mouseReleaseEvent` distinguishes:
  - **Short tap** (release before 600 ms) — emits `page_activated` exactly as before (navigate to CarPlay view, which itself ensures LIVI is running).
  - **Long press** (release after 600 ms) — emits `carplay_stop_requested`, which `MainWindow._stop_carplay` connects to `CarPlayView._stop_livi()` (kills the Electron process group via `os.killpg(SIGTERM)`).
- Visual feedback while the finger is held: the CarPlay slot dims to warm orange during the first 600 ms, then flips to `theme.NEEDLE_RED` once the threshold is crossed — "release now to stop". This is the only way the user can tell which gesture they're committing to without trial-and-error.
- The long-press handler works from any page (the sidebar is global), so the user can stop LIVI without first navigating onto the CarPlay tab. Idempotent — long-pressing again when LIVI is already dead is a no-op.

Tested in the car: held the CarPlay slot until the icon turned red, released, LIVI exited cleanly, blinking square gone. Re-tap (short) re-launched LIVI. Works.

#### Fix 2 — sidebar polarity inversion + amber tweak + red selection pip (daylight visibility)

The original sidebar had dim amber icon dots on a black background, with the selected slot inverted (filled amber background, dark dots). At noon in direct sun the un-selected slots were essentially invisible against the dashboard reflection. The selected one was readable — confirming the inversion direction was correct, just applied to the wrong slots.

Global polarity flip in `Sidebar.paintEvent`:
- **All slots** now render with the filled amber background and dark icon dots (the previous "selected" treatment).
- **Selection** is now marked by a small red pip in the top-right corner of the slot (8 px diameter, 12 px inset from the corner, `theme.NEEDLE_RED`). Period-correct vibe — looks like a tally light on a 1990s VDO control unit. The pip is suppressed during a CarPlay press hold so the slot can change colour to communicate the long-press progress without the pip mudding the signal.
- Initially also tried bumping `theme.AMBER` to `QColor(255, 191, 0)` (canonical VDO brightness yellow) for +9 % luminance on the OLED — **reverted same evening**. Two reasons captured in the in-car review: (a) the cooler yellow tone reads "wrong" against the warm-orange gauges, and (b) only `AMBER` was bumped (with `NEEDLE_AMBER` and `DOT_ON` intentionally left warmer for the round gauges), so sidebar / status bar / settings jumped to yellow while gauge needles / dot-matrix text / exit view stayed orange — a visually fractured UI. Rolled `AMBER` back to `QColor(255, 160, 30)` matching the other two constants. Single consistent warm amber across the whole UI is the right starting point; any future brightness chase needs to lift all three constants together (e.g. `(255, 170, 30)` for ~3 % more luminance with no hue shift).

In-car result: every slot is now visible at the same range in direct sun as the previously-only-bright one was — the polarity inversion was doing the heavy lifting all along, the amber tone change wasn't needed. The red pip is unambiguous against the amber fill at a glance. Net win, no regressions.

#### Fix 3 — OLED brightness control: investigated, no easy software path

Open question coming into the session: with a global brightness slider, could we tone the sidebar down at night without the user having to glance away. Three approaches probed on the Pi:

1. **DDC/CI over HDMI** — `ddcutil detect` reports "Display not found" and "This monitor does not support DDC/CI". The Waveshare 5.5" panel does not expose a DDC channel for backlight/contrast on its HDMI input. Dead end.
2. **`wlr-randr --brightness`** — installed version (0.4.1-1) does not support the `--brightness` flag. Newer wlroots have it; the Pi's apt repo doesn't ship a new enough wlr-randr. Could build from source, deferred.
3. **`gammastep`** — software brightness scaling via gamma curve. Viable fallback that would work today, but only reduces apparent brightness by clipping the high-end of each channel — not a true panel-brightness control. Acceptable for "night dimming" but doesn't help for actual OLED protection (the pixels still drive at 100 % current, just to a clipped target).

Decision: park brightness control as a future Qt-level item — `theme.AMBER` (and friends) could be wrapped in a runtime-adjusted scalar, applied at paint time. That gets us a true software dimmer with no compositor dependency, scales linearly with what's visible, and doesn't need any new package. Captured as an open follow-up; not a blocker for daily use.

Side note on the OLED itself: with all-amber sidebar fills running 24/7, burn-in is a real concern over years of use. The runtime-adjustable amber would also let us schedule a slow auto-dim during idle/night, mitigating burn-in risk. Worth implementing before the panel sees a full driving season.

#### CarPlay deep-debug — the 45-minute rabbit hole

With the long-press exit in hand, time to actually fix the underlying connect-failure root cause. Worked through it from the Pi side over SSH while in the car. The symptom: dongle present at `/sys/bus/usb/devices/3-2`, `lsusb` sees it (`1314:1520 Magic Communication Tec. Auto Box`), but `dmesg` shows the dongle disconnect-reconnect cycling every ~12 seconds. Diary entry from May 11 already documented this as the dongle's "no phone paired with me" reset loop.

Things checked along the way:

1. **Two simultaneous LIVI instances.** First `pgrep -af livi` showed two completely independent LIVI process groups, one from a prior UI session that hadn't been cleaned up on the last UI restart (the `closeEvent → os.killpg` path didn't fire because the previous shutdown was hard-killed). Both were fighting for the dongle's vendor endpoint. Used `pgrep livi` + `sudo kill -9 <pid>` to clear all of them in one shot. **Gotcha:** initial attempt with `pkill -f /tmp/.mount_LIVI` matched the `pkill` command line itself and killed the SSH shell — repaired by explicitly listing PIDs and killing by PID.
2. **Polkit `pkexec` dialog blocking LIVI startup.** LIVI tries to install `/etc/udev/rules.d/99-LIVI.rules` on first run via `pkexec`, which raises a polkit auth dialog. On this Pi the user has passwordless sudo over SSH but the polkit prompt was modal and invisible behind LIVI, so LIVI was just hanging. Worked around by:
   - Installing the udev rule directly: `sudo tee /etc/udev/rules.d/99-LIVI.rules` with the standard `SUBSYSTEM=="usb", ATTR{idVendor}=="1314", ATTR{idProduct}=="1520", MODE="0666"` content.
   - `sudo udevadm control --reload && sudo udevadm trigger`.
   - Killed the orphan `pkexec` process; `wtype` (installed for the purpose) sent a `Return` key to dismiss a follow-up "Installation Failed" dialog LIVI showed.
   - Killed LIVI entirely so the next sidebar tap would launch it cleanly with permissions already in place.
3. **Carlinkit dongle USB rebind.** `echo '3-2' | sudo tee /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/unbind && sleep 2 && echo '3-2' | sudo tee /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/bind`. Re-enumerated cleanly, but the disconnect-reconnect cycle resumed.
4. **iPhone Bluetooth status check from the Pi.** `bluetoothctl info 98:0D:AF:7B:ED:39` returned `Paired: yes / Bonded: yes / Connected: no`. So the iPhone was *not* actively BT-connected to the Pi at that moment, even though the iPhone's BT menu reportedly showed something as "Connected".
5. **WiFi scan from the Pi.** `sudo iw dev wlan0 scan` showed an SSID `LIVI` at −5 dBm (extremely strong, antenna inches away). That's the dongle's WiFi-Direct broadcast — confirms the dongle's radio is alive and broadcasting under the SSID `LIVI`.

#### Same-day addendum — BT-vs-Wi-Fi-Direct framing fix + A2DP coexistence confirmed

Two clarifications captured in the same session by running one extra in-car test.

##### BT is only the matchmaker — the actual CarPlay link is Wi-Fi-Direct

Right after the initial fix, the owner noticed the iPhone BT menu shows `LIVI — Not Connected` *while CarPlay continues to work fine*. Initially confusing — under the framing in the main entry above, "BT pair active = CarPlay up" was the implicit model, which would have made this an anomaly.

The correct model is:
- **Bluetooth role**: discovery + initial handshake only. Used once at session start to negotiate the Wi-Fi-Direct association parameters between iPhone and dongle.
- **Wi-Fi-Direct role**: carries the entire CarPlay session — video, audio, iAP2 control. Everything that actually matters once the link is up.
- **iOS power management**: once the Wi-Fi-Direct association is established, iOS routinely tears down the BT link to save phone battery. The BT entry then shows "Not Connected" until the next handshake (e.g. next ignition cycle or after a disconnect).

So `LIVI — Not Connected` in the iPhone BT menu during an active CarPlay session is **expected and normal**, not a fault. The dependable Pi-side signal remains the dongle's USB reset-cycle behaviour (cycle stops = Wi-Fi-Direct session up = CarPlay healthy), since that's tied to the actual data path, not the handshake channel.

This also rewrites the main entry's "Connected: no on the Pi → must be connected to the dongle" inference — that was still correct at the time it was made (the dongle was not yet handshaken), but the BT status alone is not a clean proxy for "is CarPlay up" in either direction.

##### A2DP to `r129` + wireless CarPlay coexist with no contention

Owner test: with CarPlay already running, opened iPhone BT menu and reconnected `r129` (the Pi's own A2DP profile, used for music streaming independent of CarPlay), then started audio playback. Two findings:

1. **No CarPlay disruption.** The dongle did not resume its reset loop, LIVI did not lose the link, the CarPlay UI kept rendering. The A2DP-to-Pi link and the Wi-Fi-Direct-to-dongle link occupy independent radios on the iPhone (BT classic for A2DP, Wi-Fi for the dongle) so they do not contend.
2. **Smooth handoff to CarPlay on next launch.** When the owner re-launched CarPlay later in the session with the YouTube app already playing on the iPhone, iOS Handoff transferred the YouTube session to CarPlay seamlessly — video to LIVI, audio routed through CarPlay's USB channel. No manual intervention needed on the phone side. This is the standard iOS Handoff path, but it's good to have confirmed in our setup.

Implications for the audio architecture work:
- The phone can be safely paired with **both** `r129` (A2DP, music when CarPlay is off) and `LIVI` (CarPlay) without managing connection state by hand. iOS picks the right radio for each.
- The audio output route is iOS's call (CarPlay USB channel vs. A2DP-to-Pi). When CarPlay is active and the user plays media, audio goes via CarPlay's USB path; if A2DP is the only sink available, it goes there instead. This means the eventual PipeWire → Match DSP routing has to handle both the USB sink (from LIVI) and the BT sink (from `bluez`) as parallel sources.
